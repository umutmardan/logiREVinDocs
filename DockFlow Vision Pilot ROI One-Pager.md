## The problem, in your dollars

Your system of record only knows what people tell it. Every day, forklifts move pallets that never get logged — and the record and the floor silently diverge:

- A picker relocates a pallet to clear a rack face, forgets to put it back. It's "in stock" in the system and invisible on the floor until cycle count.
- A truck leaves with 20 pallets against a 22-pallet BOL. You find out when the customer complains — after the driver is 400 miles away and the claim is unwinnable.
- Your security cameras saw all of it. They just can't tell you *which* aisle, *which* load, or *which* Tuesday — so finding answers means hours of scrubbing footage nobody has.

**What it costs a facility like yours (industry benchmarks):**

| Leak | Typical magnitude | Source basis |
|---|---|---|
| Inventory shrinkage | ~1.4% of on-hand inventory value is the US 3PL average; standard contracts carry a 0.5–1% shrinkage allowance you effectively absorb | Warehousing & Fulfillment 2024 survey (150 US 3PLs) |
| Warehouse shrinkage "alarm" level | WERC: >0.46% of inventory is the alarm threshold; average ~0.2% | WERC via Mecalux |
| Search labor | Workers in disorganized operations spend up to ~25% of time hunting for misplaced items | Industry studies (order-of-magnitude) |
| Lost claims | Count mismatches discovered after carrier departure are rarely recovered — no evidence, no leverage | Operator experience |

**On `[ $5,000,000 ]` average on-hand inventory, a 1% shrinkage reality is `[ $50,000 ]` a year walking out of your building** — before counting the labor spent searching, the stockouts, and the claims you couldn't fight.

## The fix: a movement ledger for your floor

We install a **standard vision kit** — cameras at the choke points every pallet must pass (dock doors, aisle ends, staging lanes), connected to an on-site edge computer — and turn your building into a system that logs what physically happens:

1. **Every pallet movement is logged** — zone to zone, timestamped, with a video clip. When a pallet is "lost" in your system, *where it actually is* becomes a 30-second lookup, not a warehouse-wide search.
2. **Forgotten pallets alert the same shift** — a pallet sitting in staging or an aisle corner too long with no job attached raises a flag while it's still cheap to fix.
3. **Count-vs-BOL mismatches flag before the driver leaves** — while the claim is still winnable.
4. **Every load gets evidence** — timestamped clips and counts, exportable as a claims bundle when a dispute lands.

**Your existing security cameras aren't touched** — they're aimed at gates and yards, not aisles. This is a separate, purpose-built layer. Raw footage stays in your building; only events and clips leave it. No face recognition, ever.
